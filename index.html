The Friction

India's Broken Payment Infrastructure

In 2013–2014, accepting online payments in India was a nightmare. Cash dominated over 95% of all transactions, and existing payment gateways were built for enterprises, not the emerging wave of internet startups and small businesses.

Weeks-Long Onboarding

Setting up a payment gateway required extensive paperwork, physical documentation, security deposits, and bank tie-ups — a process that took weeks to months with no self-serve option.

Terrible Developer Experience

Existing gateways had outdated, poorly documented APIs. Integration was cumbersome, requiring server-side redirects and complex protocols. Nothing like Stripe existed for India.

Dismal Success Rates

Payment success rates were as low as 60–70%. Transactions frequently failed due to poor redirect flows, bank page timeouts, and inadequate retry mechanisms — transaction failure rates hit 30%.

Slow Settlements

Merchants waited T+7 to T+14 days for settlement — devastating for cash-strapped startups and small businesses that needed working capital to survive.

Zero Transparency

No dashboards, no analytics, no visibility into why transactions failed. Merchants were flying blind with their payment infrastructure.

High Entry Barriers

Minimum volume requirements, setup fees, and annual maintenance charges effectively locked out startups, freelancers, and small businesses from accepting digital payments.

Who Was Affected?

Developers

Struggled with clunky, outdated payment APIs. Building e-commerce products meant wrestling with legacy integration nightmares.

Startups

The booming Indian startup ecosystem — fueled by the Flipkart era — desperately needed easy-to-integrate, affordable payment solutions.

SMBs

Small merchants wanting to go online were unable to access payment infrastructure due to high barriers, deposits, and documentation requirements.

Consumers

Faced poor checkout experiences, frequent transaction failures, multiple redirects, and limited payment choices — driving COD dominance.

The Origin Story

Harshil and Shashank met at IIT Roorkee, bonding through SDSLabs — the campus innovation lab where both were active builders. After graduating, they left their respective jobs at Schlumberger and Microsoft to pursue entrepreneurship.

Their initial plan was to build a crowdfunding platform in 2014. But while building it, they experienced firsthand the "dismal state" of online payments in India — the painful, bureaucracy-laden process of integrating a payment gateway. Recognizing this as a systemic problem affecting every Indian entrepreneur, they pivoted to build what would become Razorpay.

Operating from a small tin-roofed room in Jaipur, they faced over 100 investor rejections and enormous difficulty convincing banks to partner with an unproven fintech startup. Their break came with acceptance into Y Combinator's Winter 2015 batch — only the second India-focused startup selected at the time.

The Business Model

Full-Stack Financial Infrastructure

Razorpay evolved from a simple payment gateway into a comprehensive financial platform. Every product follows a B2B model — the merchant/business always pays, never the end consumer.

Payment Gateway

Core product enabling businesses to accept online payments via 100+ modes — UPI, cards, net banking, wallets, EMI, and BNPL. Zero setup fees, pay-as-you-go model.

2% per txn 0% UPI Merchant pays

RazorpayX — Business Banking

Neo-banking layer with current accounts, payouts API (bulk via UPI/IMPS/NEFT/RTGS), corporate cards, vendor & tax payments, and accounting integrations (Zoho, Tally). 45,000+ businesses.

Per-payout fee Business pays

RazorpayX Payroll

End-to-end automated payroll — salary disbursals, TDS/PF/ESI/PT compliance, employee self-service portal, HR tools, onboarding and exit management. 40,000+ businesses.

₹100–150/emp/mo Employer pays

Razorpay POS

In-store payment acceptance via Android Smart POS and Mini POS devices (acquired Ezetap in 2022). Unified online + offline dashboard, EMI support, GST-compliant billing.

Rental + txn fee Merchant pays

Razorpay Capital

Working capital loans and lines of credit for merchants, underwritten using their Razorpay transaction history. Collateral-free, quick digital disbursement, flexible repayment.

Interest/Fees Borrower pays

International Payments

Accept payments from 130+ currencies with automatic FIRC generation and RBI/FEMA compliance. Supports Visa, Mastercard, Amex, Apple Pay, SWIFT, ACH, SEPA.

3–3.5% per txn Merchant pays

Subscriptions & Recurring

Automates recurring billing via UPI AutoPay, cards, and e-NACH mandates. Handles plan management, failed payment retries, customer notifications, and free trial flows.

Gateway rates Merchant pays

Razorpay Route

Marketplace split-payment solution. Automatically splits incoming payments between platform and vendors with instant/deferred settlement, automated refund reversals.

Gateway rates Platform pays

The Scaling Journey

Razorpay's journey began in a modest flat in Jaipur (2014), where the co-founders pivoted from crowdfunding to solve payment access. Seeking proximity to major banking partners, they relocated to Bengaluru in late 2014, building the core engine that now captures over 55% of India's online PG market. In 2022, the company took its first global step by acquiring Curlec in Malaysia, followed by launching a regional routing hub in Singapore in 2025, transforming from a domestic startup into a Southeast Asian fintech powerhouse.

Where Razorpay Stands in 2026

From a payment gateway startup to India's most comprehensive fintech platform — Razorpay is now IPO-bound and operationally profitable, processing $180B+ annually.

Market Position

Razorpay commands approximately 55% of India's online payment gateway market, serving 80 of India's 100 unicorns and 12 million+ businesses across all segments.

  • Largest payment gateway processor in India by market share
  • Full-stack: payments + banking + lending + POS + payroll
  • 94% merchant retention rate

IPO Preparations

Razorpay filed its confidential DRHP with SEBI on June 12, 2026, targeting a listing by end of 2026.

  • Target raise: ₹5,000–6,000 crore ($500–600M) via fresh issue + OFS
  • Expected valuation: $5–6 billion
  • Bankers: Axis Capital, Kotak Mahindra Capital, J.P. Morgan, Citi
  • Completed reverse flip from US to India (May 2025)

Financial Health

FY2025 revenue of ₹3,783 crore (~$450M), up 65% year-over-year. Core online payments business is EBITDA-positive.

  • Net loss of ₹1,209 Cr largely one-time (reverse-flip tax of ₹1,275 Cr + ESOP costs)
  • Management expects consolidated profitability within 2–3 quarters
  • Annualized TPV exceeding $180 billion

Competition

While Razorpay leads the market, it competes in an increasingly competitive landscape.

  • PayU India: Strong in enterprise and subscription billing
  • Cashfree: Fast-growing in payouts and settlements
  • Juspay: Payment orchestration, powers Google Pay India
  • PhonePe PG: UPI-first, massive consumer base
  • CCAvenue: Legacy enterprise player
  • Paytm: Broad ecosystem (facing regulatory challenges)

What's Next

Razorpay's strategic focus for the coming years spans multiple growth vectors.

  • IPO: Targeting end-of-2026 listing on Indian exchanges
  • AI: Investing heavily in AI across products (Optimizer, Ray, Thirdwatch)
  • International: Expanding SEA presence — Thailand, Philippines, Vietnam
  • TPV Target: $400 billion by 2030

Recent Innovations

The company continues to push the boundaries of fintech innovation in India.

  • Razorpay Ray: AI assistant for merchants (launched 2024)
  • Turbo UPI: In-app UPI without redirect to UPI app
  • PA-P License: Physical POS payments authorization (Jan 2026)
  • Majority stake in POP: Consumer payments platform (2025)

What Razorpay Reveals About India's Fintech Ecosystem

  • UPI as Transformative Infrastructure: Razorpay was among the first to build a UPI-led gateway (2017). UPI now processes 22.7B+ monthly transactions, making India the world's largest real-time payments market.
  • The "Reverse Flip" Trend: Razorpay's redomiciling from the US to India signals maturing capital markets. India is now viable as both an operating and listing domicile for high-growth tech companies.
  • Payments as a Wedge: Razorpay evolved from a simple API to a full-stack platform (payments → banking → lending → POS → payroll) — illustrating how payments become an entry point to the entire financial services stack.
  • Regulatory Maturation: RBI's phased PA licensing created a moat for compliant players. Razorpay holds all three PA authorizations (PA-O, PA-CB, PA-P) — one of very few companies to achieve this.
  • Developer-First Wins: A Stripe-inspired, developer-first approach proved to be a massive competitive advantage in India, disrupting an industry built on relationship-based enterprise sales.
  • Growth to Discipline: The journey from VC-funded hypergrowth to operational profitability and IPO mirrors the broader maturation of India's startup ecosystem — from growth-at-all-costs to sustainable economics.
